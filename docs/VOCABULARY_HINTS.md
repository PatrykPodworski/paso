# Vocabulary memory hints

The 96 vocabulary items each have a short, individually written memory hint in `src/data/vocabulary-hints.ts`. Vocabulary feedback first states the meaning, then shows the hint separately. Hints also appear on the back of vocabulary cards and in mistake review. They are revealed after an answer or a card flip, so they do not give away a listening question beforehand.

Hints are written in English for English speakers learning Spanish. Each gives a concrete recall cue: a familiar English word, a useful translated phrase, a word breakdown, or a distinction between related Spanish words. Avoid generic instructions to picture an object, attach a label, or fill in an imaginary form. A scene is useful only when it supplies a specific connection, such as a bench outside a bank for the two meanings of banco. Word similarities are memory associations, not etymological claims. Grammar and comprehension questions retain their targeted explanations.

Linguistic references checked on 8 September 2026:

- **Silent h:** the pronunciation reminders for ordinary words such as _hola_, _hotel_ and _hablar_ follow the [RAE/ASALE DPD entry for h](https://www.rae.es/dpd/h).
- **Stress cues:** uppercase syllables identify the stressed syllable; they are a practice aid rather than a phonetic transcription. The accent reminders follow the [RAE/ASALE DPD entry for tilde](https://www.rae.es/dpd/tilde).
- **El agua:** _agua_ remains feminine, with feminine adjective agreement, despite taking _el_ immediately before its stressed initial /a/. See the [RAE/ASALE DPD entry for agua](https://www.rae.es/dpd/agua).
- **Hasta luego:** the literal breakdown uses the temporal sense of [hasta](https://dle.rae.es/hasta) and the later/afterwards sense of [luego](https://dle.rae.es/luego). The imagined future meeting is a memory cue, not a claim that the farewell promises another meeting.

Hints remain text-only. Question audio reads the Spanish teaching phrase, not the English memory hint.
